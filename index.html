Spotlight: Rivet Play's Decline

Recommendation

Priority: Rivet Play

Rivet Play is the largest account in this dataset and is actively declining, down 13 percentage points in pass rate over six months, with no corresponding rise in ticket volume. This is the exact scenario that goes unnoticed until the customer raises it, since ticket monitoring alone shows nothing unusual.

Foundry Gaming and Tidewater Casino both show structurally high review rates, but for different reasons: Foundry's tickets skew heavily toward Compliance Decision Friction, while Tidewater's skew toward Operational Backlog Friction. Both are worth a closer look, but neither is actively getting worse, so they are less urgent than Rivet Play's silent decline.

Why not a category-first approach: tackling User Verification Friction across all eight customers would help everyone a little, but would not have caught Rivet Play's decline at all, since that decline shows up in the funnel data, not the ticket data.

Methodology Notes

  • Support tickets were categorized using an exact-match lookup against 50 fixed subject line templates, not free-text NLP, since the subject field is a closed set rather than open text.
  • Ticket volume was normalized per 1,000 sessions per customer, since raw ticket counts scale with account size and would otherwise overstate friction for larger customers like Rivet Play.
  • Health status (red/amber/green) is rule-based on trend and review rate thresholds, not a single blended score, so the reasoning behind each status stays visible rather than hidden inside a formula.
  • Analysis covers a 6-month window (Feb–Aug 2026); trend comparisons use the first 4 weeks vs. the last 4 weeks of that window.

Next Steps

  • Rivet Play: work with Solutions Engineering to pinpoint why doc_pass_rate specifically has slipped, since that's the exact stage where Rivet's funnel diverges from every other customer.
  • Foundry Gaming: review whether compliance rule tuning is driving the high share of PEP/sanctions disputes, this looks like a policy conversation, not a technical one.
  • Tidewater Casino: check review-queue staffing or throughput, since their friction is concentrated in backlog, not disputed decisions.
  • Roll this view out weekly for Support and Solutions Engineering, so a slide like Rivet's gets caught in week 3, not month 6.

Keeping This Current

Right now this is a snapshot, built from a CSV export at a point in time. Making it run itself would mean three things:

  • What refreshes: a scheduled job re-pulls the funnel and ticket data straight from source, then recalculates the rate axis, review rate, six-month trend, and ticket category breakdown for every customer, the same logic already built here.
  • On what trigger: weekly, on a fixed schedule, rather than someone remembering to re-export and re-open this file.
  • How it flags: since the red/amber/green status is already explicit trend and review-rate thresholds, not a hidden formula, the job can just check whether any customer crossed a threshold since last week, and flag it, for example, through a Slack alert to Support and Solutions Engineering, instead of waiting for someone to notice on the next manual review.
To be upfront: this part isn't built. This file is a static snapshot and can't schedule itself or call Slack on its own. Actually running this would mean a scheduled Databricks job and a Slack webhook, sitting outside this file entirely. What's here is the design, and the reason it's a short list is that the hard part, the actual health-status logic, is already built and already explicit enough to automate.
